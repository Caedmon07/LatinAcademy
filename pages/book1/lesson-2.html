The First Conjugation

You already know how amō changes its ending. Now use the same pattern to conjugate several first-conjugation verbs.

⏱ About 15 minutes ⭐ 35 XP 🏛 Present tense

1 · ONE RELIABLE PATTERN

Six endings do most of the work

The present-tense ending identifies person and number. Learn the sequence and you can apply it to many verbs.

MEMORY CHANT

-ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt

2 · FIND THE STEM

Remove the final -ō

For these regular first-conjugation verbs, the dictionary form ends in -ō. The present stem normally contains a long ā.

cantō → cantā- sing
vocō → vocā- call
nāvigō → nāvigā- sail

7 · PRESENT-TENSE ENGLISH

One Latin form, several natural translations

amō

I love

I am loving

I do love

cantat

he sings

she is singing

it does sing

Translation principle

Choose the English form that sounds natural in context. Latin does not need separate words equivalent to English am or do in these present-tense translations.

8 · LESSON SUMMARY

Your first-conjugation toolkit

✓ First-conjugation verbs share the same present-tense endings.

✓ The ending sequence is -ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt.

✓ The present stem usually ends in long ā.

✓ Person and number come from the ending.

✓ The same pattern works with cantō, vocō, nāvigō, aedificō and others.
