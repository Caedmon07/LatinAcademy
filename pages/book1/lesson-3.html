BOOK I · CHAPTER 1 · LESSON 3

Future and Imperfect

Latin uses recognisable tense markers to show whether an action will happen or was continuing in the past.

⏱ About 18 minutes ⭐ 45 XP ⏳ Two new tenses

1 · THINK ABOUT TIME

Now, later or continuing in the past?

Compare the time expressed by each English sentence.

NOW

I love

Present tense

LATER

I will love

Future tense

PAST, CONTINUING

I was loving

Imperfect tense

Which sentence describes a continuing action in the past?

2 · THE FUTURE TENSE

Look for -bō, -bis, -bit…

The future endings attach to the present stem and tell us who will act.

FUTURE CHANT

-bō, -bis, -bit, -bimus, -bitis, -bunt

3 · THE IMPERFECT TENSE

Look for -bam, -bās, -bat…

The imperfect describes an action that was happening, continued or used to happen.

IMPERFECT CHANT

-bam, -bās, -bat, -bāmus, -bātis, -bant

4 · COMPARE THE MARKERS

Future or imperfect?

Identify the tense from the marker before translating the person and action.

FUTURE amā-bit

he, she or it will love

IMPERFECT amā-bat

he, she or it was loving / used to love

7 · AND, BUT, NOT

Connect and negate ideas

9 · LESSON SUMMARY

Two tense systems

✓ Future: -bō, -bis, -bit, -bimus, -bitis, -bunt.

✓ Imperfect: -bam, -bās, -bat, -bāmus, -bātis, -bant.

✓ Future means an action will happen.

✓ Imperfect means an action was happening or used to happen.

✓ nōn is placed before the verb; et joins with “and”; sed contrasts with “but”.
