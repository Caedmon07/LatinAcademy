BOOK I · CHAPTER 1 · LESSON 4

Principal Parts

Latin verbs are learned through a small set of key forms. These forms provide the stems needed to build the wider verb system.

⏱ About 18 minutes⭐ 50 XP📜 Four key forms

1 · WHY FOUR FORMS?

A verb has more than one useful stem

The dictionary form alone cannot supply every tense. Principal parts act like a compact construction kit.

1

Present meaning

amō tells you the basic meaning: “I love”.

2

Present system

amāre supplies the present stem amā-.

3

Perfect system

amāvī supplies the perfect stem amāv-.

4

Later constructions

amātum supplies a further stem used later.

2 · MEET THE FOUR PARTS

amō, amāre, amāvī, amātum

Listen to each form and learn what information it provides.

FULL SEQUENCE

amō · amāre · amāvī · amātum

3 · FIND THE PRESENT STEM

Use the second principal part

Remove -re from the infinitive to reveal the present stem.

amāre− reamā-

Which form gives the present stem of nāvigō?

4 · FIND THE PERFECT STEM

Use the third principal part

Remove final -ī from the third principal part.

amāvī− īamāv-

What is the perfect stem of cantāvī?

5 · THE REGULAR PATTERN

One pattern, many verbs

Regular first-conjugation verbs commonly follow -ō, -āre, -āvī, -ātum.
