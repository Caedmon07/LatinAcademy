# Welcome to Latin

**Salvē!**

Latin began as the language of ancient Rome. As Roman power expanded, Latin spread through much of Europe, North Africa and the eastern Mediterranean.

Although Latin is no longer spoken as a native language, it continued for centuries as a language of education, law, government, religion, science and literature. Its influence remains visible today.

## What is Latin?

Latin was the language of the Roman Republic and later the Roman Empire. It was used in public life, administration, law, the army, trade and literature.

After the western Roman Empire declined, Latin continued to be used by scholars, clergy, lawyers, diplomats and scientists. It became one of the principal written languages of medieval and early modern Europe.

## Why Latin matters

Latin is useful because it helps learners:

- understand English vocabulary more deeply
- recognise patterns in French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Romanian
- interpret scientific and medical terminology
- understand legal expressions
- study Roman history, literature and inscriptions
- develop close reading and analytical skills

## Latin and English

English is not descended directly from Latin, but Latin has contributed a very large number of English words.

Some words entered English through French after the Norman Conquest. Others came directly through education, religion, law, science and scholarship.

### Example: portāre

Latin **portāre** means “to carry”.

English words derived from this root include:

- portable
- transport
- import
- export
- porter

### Example: scrībere

Latin **scrībere** means “to write”.

Related English words include:

- scribe
- describe
- prescribe
- inscription
- manuscript

## The Romance languages

Several modern languages developed directly from varieties of spoken Latin. These are called the Romance languages.

The main Romance languages include:

- Italian
- Spanish
- Portuguese
- French
- Romanian
- Catalan

### Word comparison

| Meaning | Latin | Italian | Spanish | French |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| water | aqua | acqua | agua | eau |
| mother | māter | madre | madre | mère |
| new | novus | nuovo | nuevo | nouveau |

The words are not identical, but their family resemblance is often visible.

## Latin is still around us

Many familiar words and expressions come directly from Latin:

- video
- audio
- campus
- data
- agenda
- bonus
- memorandum
- alibi
- vice versa
- per se
- ad hoc

## Latin expression

### sine quā nōn

Literally: “without which, not”.

It means something essential or indispensable.

## Summary

By the end of this lesson, you should understand that:

- Latin was the language of Rome
- Latin remained important long after the Roman Empire
- Latin strongly influenced English vocabulary
- the Romance languages developed from Latin
- Latin remains important in science, medicine, law and education
